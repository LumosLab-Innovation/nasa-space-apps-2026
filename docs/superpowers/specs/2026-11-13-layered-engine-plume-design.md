## Intent and agreed direction

Replace the pale, static cone beneath the HLS with believable, animated exhaust. The user chose **moderate prominence** and **A: an animated core combined with layered particles**, with transparent **300 × 300 WebP** artwork. The effect should communicate changing thrust without overwhelming the spacecraft. The browser companion is an illustrative motion study, not footage from the game and not a physics simulation.

This is one bounded visual-effects feature spanning Orion separation, orbit, final descent, asset preparation and QA. No gameplay tuning is needed. The Orion addition reuses the same effect resources with its own small auxiliary-engine pulse envelope.

## Approaches

- **A — animated core plus particle layers (chosen).** A lightweight procedural shader gives the plume continuous internal motion and soft boundaries; alpha-textured particles supply irregular outer wisps and surface dust. More integration work, but holds its shape at oblique angles with fewer overlapping sprites.
- **B — particles only.** Easier to author as transparent sprite artwork, but close/end-on views can expose flat cards and require extra overdraw. Rejected in favor of A.

## Visual design

1. **Core:** short white-blue region directly at each active nozzle. Brightest at the exit, fading along the flow, with no hard opaque outline or uniform solid fill.
2. **Flow:** pale blue with a restrained violet tint, moving texture detail traveling away from the engine. Irregular soft edges; never a solid triangular silhouette. In vacuum the outer flow broadens and becomes faint rather than forming a long opaque flame.
3. **Outer wisps:** low-opacity alpha sprites with varied size, rotation and lifetime. They break up the tail without forming distinct floating balls or showing square texture edges.
4. **Glow:** a small soft contribution around the nozzle/core. Keep spacecraft panel detail visible. No change to scene-wide exposure, bloom or Earth lighting.
5. **Surface response:** near the ground, the existing ejected dust becomes a thin, outward-moving, terrain-following sheet with intermittent grains. No continuously rising smoke column or decorative shower of hot sparks. Dust keeps moving ballistically after engine cutoff; new emission stops.
6. **Near-ground exhaust deflection (user addition):** as the downward plume reaches the ground, its faint luminous outer flow turns outward along the surface. It appears to fan left and right in the side view, but spreads radially in the actual 3D scene. Keep the soft flame look; do not add a flat glowing disc, a wall of fire, or hot sparks. This is gas impingement/deflection, not atmospheric drag. Reduce the luminous spread radius and intensity continuously with throttle, then extinguish it on cutoff. Dust is a separate effect and persists independently.

Brightness is moderate at the approved close camera: core visible against black sky and gray terrain, outer plume translucent, spacecraft remains the visual subject. The mockup uses a simplified ship solely to establish this balance.

## Motion and throttle

- Smooth ignition growth over roughly 0.3–0.5 s and ordinary throttle changes over 0.1–0.2 s. Presentation smoothing must not feed back into acceleration, fuel use or autopilot.
- Use continuous band-limited noise for approximately ±6% brightness and ±4% length variation, with independently seeded nozzles. No per-frame random scaling, flashing all engines together, or frame-rate dependent flicker.
- Clarified revision: preserve the original soft core, color, transparency, subtle texture motion and edge shape. Vary each nozzle's visible plume LENGTH independently instead of ending all plumes on the same plane. Use small differing baseline lengths and smooth independently seeded temporal variation (illustrative starting multipliers 0.84, 1.03, 0.94 for the three visible preview nozzles, each with ±12% continuous variation). Map the actual six nozzles proportionately to their geometry and keep the total effect moderately prominent. This is visual modulation only, not a change to individual engine forces. Do not add lightning-like filaments, stronger raggedness, extra lateral waving, or a new color/brightness style. These length variations supersede the earlier ±4% length starting value.
- Lower throttle shortens and dims the effect; zero throttle before contact stops emission and fades luminous residue within roughly 0.1–0.2 s. CONTACT instead uses the explicitly requested hold-and-decay envelope below.
- Animate using a pause-aware visual clock in real playback seconds. Automatic flight's ×10 simulation rate must not accelerate flame noise tenfold. Pausing freezes core motion, particles and dust, and resuming does not catch up with a burst.
- Near 300 m there is exhaust but no ground dust. Preserve the existing 30 m dust trigger for this feature; make its intensity ramp continuous rather than changing landing rules.
- Ground-light spread must use each nozzle's actual distance to rendered terrain (the nozzle-to-ground clearance is not necessarily the HUD's vehicle reference altitude). The builder may use a local surface adapter with interpolated height/normal samples; avoid per-particle/per-frame scene raycasts and new full-scene lights.
- The downward visible plume terminates at the terrain and blends into the outward flow instead of disappearing through the ground. Radius and opacity depend continuously on proximity and visual throttle; no fixed altitude toggle or sudden rectangular clipping edge. Collision/visibility tests must include uneven ground and all actual nozzle locations.
- Latest user correction replaces the previous immediate/short contact fade: keep a clearly visible lateral fan briefly AFTER touchdown, then shrink and fade it gradually. Builder-proposed timings illustrated in v8 are **0.4 s hold after CONTACT, followed by 0.8 s eased decay**. Capture the current visual throttle at contact and decay from that value; do not reignite a plume that was already off. Fan extent and opacity both decay, with an exact zero endpoint, rather than leaving an expanding luminous ring.
- This contact tail is a cosmetic presentation with the landed state already latched. It does not continue propulsion forces, consume extra fuel or change touchdown physics. During a nonzero contact-tail envelope, the visual ground interaction may emit a declining bounded amount of dust; stop new emission at the envelope endpoint while existing grains continue ballistically. Freeze the entire sequence on pause and dispose it on scene exit/retry.
- The lateral fan must be visibly broader/brighter than v7 at the unchanged gameplay camera: a clear soft horizontal flow on both sides of the vehicle in side view, while still below the core's brightness. Suggested extent is roughly 2–3 landing-gear footprint diameters total at peak, tuned against slope/occlusion; no uniform opaque band. Ground deflection should be spatially irregular in 3D, and must not produce a symmetric flat disc. The companion's repeated descent/taper is an illustrative timeline, not a new flight controller.
- Preserve the plume time/seed, smoothed throttle and live particle state during the orbit-to-descent overlap. The incoming scene must not restart its ignition, double the visible plume brightness, or lose the existing tail at the handoff. A common simulation state with two scene render adapters is the preferred implementation; the builder may use equivalent state transfer if it preserves the full visible state.
