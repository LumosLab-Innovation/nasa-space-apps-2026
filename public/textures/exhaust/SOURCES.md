# Exhaust artwork and physical references

The four 300 × 300 RGBA WebPs (glow, wisp, dust, grain) are original procedural alpha fields generated by `scripts/build-exhaust-textures.py`. Lossless WebP, 35,750 bytes total. No frames, textures or artwork were extracted from the references.

Visual references:
- NASA STS-6 aft reaction-control imagery: https://www.nasa.gov/history/40-years-ago-first-flight-of-space-shuttle-challenger/
- NASA SVS Roman attitude-control animation (animation, not flight footage): https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14573/
- SpaceX SN15 atmospheric landing footage: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9eoubnO-pE&t=736s
- NASA plume/surface interaction experiments: https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-begins-moon-mission-plume-surface-interaction-tests/
- ESA Orion propulsion: https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Orion/European_Service_Module_Propulsion

Interpretation: compact paired attitude jets; main exhaust expands/dissipates in vacuum and spreads along the surface on impingement. No atmospheric smoke column or decorative sparks. The visible cool glow, pulse timings, inferred HLS RCS port placement and 0.4 s contact hold + 0.8 s fade are game art, not calibrated radiation, certified hardware or measured shutdown timing. Orion uses the model's eight small aft auxiliaries, separately from the unlit central engine.
